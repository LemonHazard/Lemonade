- Reloading while the Visual Studio debugger is attached will also need Casey’s unique-PDB-name trick, which isn’t in yet.
- Meshes are copied to the GPU the first time they're drawn and kept until exit, recognized by their data's address: vertices or indices changed in place aren't picked up, nothing is freed, and past 4096 meshes or 128 MiB of them new ones aren't drawn. A frame uploads at most 16 MiB of new meshes and textures: the rest show up a frame or more later, and a single mesh bigger than that is never drawn. Each of these limits logs a warning once.
- Only Linux draws anything: the Vulkan renderer is Linux-only, so Windows and macOS build with the null renderer. macOS has no window code at all yet.
- The Windows window doesn't read the keyboard or mouse, so key bindings do nothing there.
- Gamepads aren't read yet, although input.h already lists their keys.
- Assets are only loaded at startup: editing a .glb or key_bindings.kvl needs a restart, unlike code changes.
- Textures are copied to the GPU the first time they're drawn and kept until exit, recognized by their pixels' address: pixels changed in place aren't picked up, nothing is freed, and past 1024 textures their defaults are drawn instead (white, or flat normals). Each different set of a material's textures gets a descriptor set, also kept until exit: past 1024 of them, meshes with new ones aren't drawn. A texture bigger than a frame's 16 MiB of uploads is copied over several frames, and meshes drawn with it don't show until it's done: a 4096x4096 one takes 4 frames.
- The sun's shadows come from one 2048x2048 shadow map over a sphere the game places: no cascades, so a big view gets blurry shadows, or none past the sphere. Without the device's depth clamping, what's between the sun and the sphere casts no shadow into it.
- Materials' alpha isn't used: glass and other transparent or cut-out surfaces are drawn opaque.
